1

Match clock. Starts when you tap Start match and never stops. It is the only timing the prototype keeps — no per-player chess clock yet.

2

Whose turn, and the move number. It flips itself after every move you log. More → Hand the turn over skips a side when a rule says someone loses their go.

3

The board, in two taps. Tap the piece you just moved on the wood, then the square it landed on. Any piece can go anywhere — the console never refuses a move, because your rules will be the ones deciding that.

4

Gold ring, and the dots. The ring is the piece you picked up. The dots are where ordinary chess would let it go — a guide for the eye while you learn the terrain, switched off under More.

5

Terrain marks. Copy your printed map onto the grid once, at the start. Every move then records what it stepped off and what it stepped onto.

6

Cards in play. Give it a name and a length. It counts down one step per move and falls off by itself. Tap it to pull it early.

7

The record, newest at the top. Flags and a note sit beside the move. Undo replays the whole match from the opening position, so the board and the record can never drift apart.
